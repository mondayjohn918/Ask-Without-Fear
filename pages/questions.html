Common Questions

Is it normal for a Christian to struggle with lust?

Yes, struggling with temptation is something almost every believer faces. Being tempted is not the same as sinning. The Bible says Jesus himself was tempted in every way, yet did not sin, and that we can come to him for mercy and help when we struggle.

The Bible also says that no temptation is unique to you, and that God provides a way out. If you have fallen, confession brings forgiveness, not condemnation. You are not too broken to ask for help.

Scripture: Hebrews 4:15-16, 1 Corinthians 10:13, 1 John 1:9

How do I know God's will for my life?

God's will is not usually a secret code to crack. Scripture points us to trust him, give our lives to him, and ask him for wisdom. He promises to give wisdom generously to anyone who asks.

Start with what is already clear: loving God, loving people, staying faithful in small things and seeking wise counsel from mature believers. Direction often becomes clearer as we walk.

Scripture: Proverbs 3:5-6, Romans 12:1-2, James 1:5

Can a Christian date someone who isn't a Christian?

The Bible does not speak about modern dating directly, so we look at its wider principles. Scripture warns believers about being closely bound to unbelievers in ways that pull them in different directions, and marriage is the deepest example of that.

Ask honestly: can two people with different foundations walk together toward the same goal? Pray about it, and talk with a trusted mature Christian before making the decision.

Scripture: 2 Corinthians 6:14, Amos 3:3

Why does God allow suffering?

This is one of the hardest questions, and the Bible does not give a simple answer to every part of it. What it does show is that God is close to people who are hurting, and that Jesus wept with those who grieved.

The book of Job shows that honest questions and even complaints can be brought to God. Scripture promises that God is with us in pain and that suffering will not have the last word.

Scripture: Psalm 34:18, John 11:35, John 16:33, Romans 8:28

How do I know if I'm ready for marriage?

Readiness is less about age or strong feelings and more about character and commitment. The Bible describes marriage as two people becoming one, and as a love that is willing to sacrifice for the other person.

Ask honestly: Am I growing in my walk with God? Can I handle conflict maturely? Am I responsible with money and time? Wise, mature believers who know you well can help you see what you might miss.

Scripture: Genesis 2:24, Ephesians 5:25, Proverbs 15:22

What if it feels like God isn't answering my prayers?

Many people in the Bible felt this way. David asked God how long he would feel forgotten. Silence does not mean God is absent or angry, and honest prayer, including frustrated prayer, is welcome.

Scripture encourages us to keep asking and to bring everything to God, trusting that he hears us even when the answer is not what, or when, we expected.

Scripture: Psalm 13:1-2, Matthew 7:7-8, Philippians 4:6-7
